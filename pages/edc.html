Tl;Dr

UniWeb wanted to take a bespoke EDC tool built for one client and turn it into a scalable SaaS product for the clinical trials market. I was the first - and only - UX designer on the project for approximately a year. I owned everything: requirements gathering, use-case diagramming, a full design system, interactive prototypes in Axure, and documentation that had to meet Good Manufacturing Practice standards. The stakes were unusually high: bad UX in a clinical trial platform doesn't just frustrate users, it compromises data integrity.

The Story

Designing for a domain where errors have consequences

Electronic Data Capture systems are used by clinical trial coordinators to log patient data - precisely, consistently, and in a way that holds up to regulatory audit. The existing tool worked for DICE-CRO, the long-term client it was built around. The ambition was something reusable: a platform that required little or no custom coding for each new trial.

My starting point was understanding the workflows deeply enough to know where UX could reduce friction without compromising rigour. That meant sitting with the clinical data, not just the interface requirements.

The constraint that focused everything: every design decision had to be documented to GMP standards. That discipline turned out to be useful - it forced clarity I might have otherwise skipped.

Building the design system alongside the product

Leadership had mandated Google Material Design as the foundation - a reasonable call for a form-heavy platform where consistency matters more than novelty. I took that mandate and built a proper design system on top of it: components, patterns, states, and documentation that a future team could actually use.

The system had to handle a wide range of complexity - from simple data entry fields to multi-layered audit trails and permission structures. I worked through each feature area methodically: data entry, monitoring, query generation, randomisation, subject PDF generation, and more.

Prototyping and testing - with the tools available

I built fully interactive prototypes in Axure RP 8 - the level of fidelity the platform needed to meaningfully test complex workflows. Formal usability testing wasn't always possible given internal prioritisation pressures (internal projects often came second to paying clients), so I ran guerrilla testing wherever I could to catch the most critical issues.

It wasn't a perfect research environment. But imperfect feedback is better than none, and I was pragmatic about using what I had access to.

Interactive Axure prototype walkthrough.

What the platform was designed to do

The feature scope was broad by necessity - clinical trial management touches many stakeholders with very different needs. The platform covered real-time data entry with validation, source data verification, query generation, custom reporting, randomisation, double data entry, training modules, a full audit trail, and user/site administration. Every feature needed to be both usable and audit-ready.
